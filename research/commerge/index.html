Abstract

Multi-robot LiDAR map merging enables faster exploration and efficient area coverage by maintaining a global consistency across robot teams. However, this requires exchanging massive sensory data between robots, creating a fundamental bottleneck in communication-constrained environments. We present Commerge, a hybrid framework that reduces inter-robot data exchange by 1000x while maintaining alignment accuracy. Our key insight is that only a small subset of carefully selected scans is necessary for robust map merging. We formulate this selection as a progressive optimization on an exchange graph, where vertices represent robot keyframes and edges denote candidate inter-robot loops. Through three stages—sequential matching for temporal coherence, balanced minimum vertex cover for complete coverage with minimal transmission, and maximum edge-weighted clique for geometric consistency—we identify the minimal scan subset that preserves inter-robot alignment quality. Unlike existing approaches that transmit complete datasets (GB-scale) or employ naive downsampling, Commerge exchanges only MB-scale data while achieving comparable or superior alignment accuracy. Extensive evaluation on six datasets shows 99.98% reduction in data exchange (from 7.0 GB to 1.7 MB on HeLiPR), while maintaining real-time performance across embedded to desktop platforms. The framework successfully operates on resource-constrained hardware where existing methods fail, enabling practical multi-robot deployment in bandwidth-limited field operations.
