Abstract

By maintaining global consistency across robot teams, multi-robot LiDAR map merging enables faster exploration and efficient area coverage. However, map merging requires exchanging massive sensor data between the server and robots, making communication the bottleneck, especially in communication-constrained environments.

Therefore, we present Commerge, a communication-efficient map merging framework that achieves bandwidth reduction through graph-theoretic selective data exchange. Our key insight is that only a small subset of carefully selected scans is sufficient for robust map merging. We formulate this as a three-stage cascaded optimization problem on an exchange graph, where vertices represent robot keyframes and edges denote candidate inter-robot loops. Through three cascade stages, we select a sequentially overlapped, balanced-transmission-cost, and geometrically-perceptually optimal scan subset that preserves alignment quality while reducing communication.

Unlike existing approaches that either transmit whole scans requiring GB-scale data exchange, or employ naive downsampling, our approach exchanges only MB-scale data while achieving comparable alignment accuracy. Extensive evaluation on five public datasets and four in-house datasets covering cave, planetary-analog, indoor, and outdoor campus environments shows up to 99.98% reduction in data exchange (e.g., from 7,000MB to 1.3MB on the HeLiPR dataset), while maintaining alignment performance across embedded to desktop platforms.

Motivation

Multi-robot LiDAR map merging keeps a robot team globally consistent, but every merge decision rests on raw scan exchange between robots and the server. In bandwidth-limited field settings — caves, planetary-analog sites, disaster zones — transmitting whole scans is infeasible, while naive downsampling discards exactly the geometry needed for reliable alignment.

Commerge targets this gap: instead of sending everything or blindly subsampling, it reasons about which scans are worth transmitting, exchanging only an MB-scale subset that preserves merge accuracy.

What Commerge adds

Graph-theoretic exchange

An exchange graph whose vertices are robot keyframes and edges are candidate inter-robot loops turns "what to transmit" into a principled selection problem instead of ad-hoc downsampling.

Three-stage cascade

A cascaded optimization selects a sequentially overlapped, transmission-cost-balanced, and geometrically–perceptually optimal scan subset that preserves alignment quality.

MB-scale communication

Up to 99.98% reduction in data exchange (e.g., 7,000 MB → 1.3 MB on HeLiPR), reaching up to 5,000× lower inter-robot communication.

Embedded to desktop

Robust, fast merging validated on five public and four in-house datasets across cave, planetary-analog, indoor, and outdoor campus environments, from embedded to desktop platforms.

Pipeline

Commerge builds an exchange graph over robot keyframes and candidate inter-robot loops, then runs a three-stage cascaded optimization to pick a minimal, informative scan subset. Only the selected scans are transmitted and used for robust map merging on the server.

Exchange graph

Vertices are per-robot keyframes; edges denote candidate inter-robot loop closures that could align the maps.

Selective transmission

Only scans chosen by the cascade cross the network, cutting exchange from GB-scale to MB-scale.

Robust merging

The server aligns received scans to recover a globally consistent multi-robot map.

Platform-agnostic

The same pipeline runs from embedded compute to desktop, keeping merging fast under tight resource budgets.

Three-Stage Cascaded Selection

Scan selection is formulated as a cascaded optimization on the exchange graph. Each stage refines the candidate subset, so the final selection is simultaneously well-overlapped, cheap to transmit, and geometrically informative.

1 · Sequential overlap

Select scans that stay sequentially overlapped along each trajectory, so the transmitted subset still covers the shared structure needed for alignment.

2 · Balanced cost

Balance the transmission cost across robots and links so no single channel dominates the exchange budget.

3 · Geometric–perceptual

Keep the geometrically and perceptually most informative scans, preserving alignment quality with the fewest bytes.

Evaluation

Commerge is evaluated on five public datasets and four in-house datasets spanning cave, planetary-analog, indoor, and outdoor campus environments, on platforms ranging from embedded to desktop. We report both communication cost and map-merging accuracy.
